1. triage_model.pkl
What it is: The trained Machine Learning model (HistGradientBoostingClassifier).

What it contains: All the mathematical rules, decision tree splits, and branch weights learned from the emergency room dataset.

What it does in the backend:

Accepts the structured array of patient vitals and labs.

Calculates the exact probability (0% to 100%) of a patient being critical.

Outputs the final prediction: HIGH ACUITY (Urgent) or LOW ACUITY (Routine).

2. chief_complaint_map.pkl
What it is: A target-encoding lookup dictionary for categorical chief complaints.

What it contains: Key-value pairs mapping textual complaints to risk probabilities (e.g., {"Chest pain": 0.482, "Sprain": 0.051, "Cold symptoms": 0.081}).

What it does in the backend:

Machine learning models cannot process plain text strings like "Chest pain" directly.

When a user submits a chief complaint text, the backend looks it up in this dictionary and converts it into a numerical risk score for the model.

3. global_medians.pkl
What it is: A dictionary of global baseline medians for every vital sign and lab test.

What it contains: Median values calculated from the training data (e.g., {"systolic_bp": 128.0, "troponin": 0.0, "spo2": 96.6}).

What it does in the backend:

In real emergency rooms, patients often arrive before lab results (like troponin or bnp) are ready.

If the incoming request has missing vital signs or pending lab values, the backend uses this file to automatically fill in the missing numbers with realistic medians, preventing the model from crashing.
